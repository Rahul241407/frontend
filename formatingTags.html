A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (15 October 1931 – 27 July 2015) was an Indian aerospace scientist and the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007.

He was born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, and studied aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.

Kalam played a major role in India's space and missile development programmes. He was popularly known as the "Missile Man of India".

Early Life

Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, India.

His father, Jainulabdeen, was a boat owner and imam of a local mosque. His mother, Ashiamma, was a housewife.

Kalam came from a humble family and worked hard during his childhood to support his family.

Education

Kalam studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli.

Later, he studied aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.

Career

Kalam joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) after completing his education.

He later joined the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). He worked on India's first satellite launch vehicle SLV.

He also played an important role in India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme.

Presidency

Abdul Kalam became the 11th President of India on 25 July 2002.

His presidency lasted until 25 July 2007.

He was popularly known as the "People's President" because of his connection with students and young people.

Books

Kalam was also a successful author. Some of his famous books include:

  • Wings of Fire
  • India 2020
  • Ignited Minds
  • My Journey

Awards and Honours

Abdul Kalam received many awards for his contributions to science and public service.

  • Padma Bhushan – 1981
  • Padma Vibhushan – 1990
  • Bharat Ratna – 1997

Death

Abdul Kalam died on 27 July 2015 after suffering a cardiac arrest while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong.

He was 83 years old.
